The problem

Zi Pay is a points-based payment wallet meant to make everyday spending simpler and more rewarding for individuals—while helping small businesses grow sales through loyalty.

A Canadian banker asked me to turn his idea into a prototype he could show stakeholders in a pitch: a points-based mobile wallet people would use to manage day-to-day spending and collect rewards from local businesses.

Planning & strategy

It was a three-week engagement, and the business side was still thin. I planned a one-week Lean Stack session to build a workable business model first, then advised hiring a junior UX researcher so research could run in parallel. That was the only realistic way to recover lost time and still finish on schedule and on budget.

Lean business model

I researched and facilitated the Lean business model with the client, and coached him on how to keep iterating it with continuous innovation after the engagement. I also built the pitch deck from a ready-made Excel template and shared it on Google Drive so stakeholders could collaborate easily.

Researching users & businesses

Building the business model clarified users, competitors, industry patterns, and the market. I reconvened with stakeholders to refine requirements with that evidence, then built personas around needs, current workarounds, stated wants, unknown wants, and what Zi Pay could uniquely do.

I started with an online survey for small, necessity-based businesses—barber shops, convenience stores, carpenters, and similar—focused on how they win and keep customers.

For consumers, I ran a short focus group with the stakeholder’s family and friends: daily spending choices, businesses they visit often, savings and spending apps, and which points systems they actually liked. From that session I distilled five key questions into a Google Form, targeted segments we still lacked data on, and used an Amazon gift card incentive to get results within 48 hours.

Customer decision path

Using the focus group and survey data, I mapped the customer decision path—how someone discovers a reward, joins, spends, and comes back. That path sharpened which features belonged in the first prototype and which could wait.

Feature set & platform choice

I synthesised market, customer, and business findings, then compared them against the technology needed to ship. With the client we agreed to design an iOS-first prototype—driven mainly by presentation quality. The immediate need was a polished pitch demo that could impress investors, not a multi-platform build.

Information architecture

After locking the feature set with the stakeholder, I built the site map and primary user flows so wallet, rewards, offers, and social sharing sat in a clear hierarchy.

UI system, wireframes & design

Working from iOS UI patterns, I built a quick style guide, then wireframed the app and evolved those frames into high-fidelity screens for the pitch prototype.

Business use case

For merchants, Zi Pay connects through points-based reward cards. Businesses subscribe to reward-card offers, get guided support on acquisition and retention, and receive managed rewards data plus monthly analytics so they can keep improving.

Customer use case

From customer feedback, the must-have consumer journey was simple: discover reward cards through offers or search, add them to an account, and watch rewards accumulate as they spend.

End result

The pitch-ready iOS prototype brought the two sides together: consumers could find companies, claim reward cards, get prompted after a purchase, review purchase history, and share spending with friends—features chosen to create a reason to return, not only to collect points once.

The client left with a Lean canvas, a collaborative pitch deck, and a high-fidelity prototype strong enough to present to investors.
